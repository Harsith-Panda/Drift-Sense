# R2 viva preparation

1. Why a 1D-CNN at all? The window is a short multichannel time series. A convolution slides one small detector along time, so a spike or a brief oscillation can be recognised without a feature being written for it.
2. What is the receptive field? Kernels 7, 5 and 3 at stride 1 give 13 samples, 65 ms at 200 Hz. Global average pooling, not the field, is what lets a detection anywhere in the 2 s window affect the embedding. One filter does not see the whole window.
3. Why is the LSTM embedding the last hidden state? That state is the only representation that has been updated by all 400 steps. A pool of outputs would throw away the order the recurrence was added to keep.
4. Why are the heads the same class? E1 and E2 differ in the encoder. The map from 64 numbers to the drift target is one linear layer, with 1 output for these runs and 4 when a later experiment needs the axis errors.
5. How many parameters? CNN encoder 24,352, full model 24,417. LSTM encoder 18,432, full model 18,497. The counts are tested, not estimated from a diagram.
6. Which loss, optimiser, and schedule? Mean squared error, AdamW with decoupled weight decay 0.0001, initial learning rate 0.001. ReduceLROnPlateau halves the rate after five validation epochs without an improvement of 1e-5. Early stopping patience is 15. Seed 42.
7. Why refresh batch-norm statistics? Dropout sits after batch-norm inside each block, so the next block is normalised on dropped activations and then used, at prediction time, on full ones. The refresh is a forward pass over the training windows only, with dropout off, before validation and before the checkpoint is written.
8. Did the CNN overfit? Yes, in the ordinary sense. Training error keeps falling after validation has stopped improving. On the MH_02 training fold the restored epoch is epoch 2. Validation rises into a plateau near 11 cm; it does not diverge. The saved weights are that early epoch. Test MAE is 10.2 cm and 17.5 cm, and $R^2$ is negative.
9. Did the LSTM overfit in the same way? No. Validation MAE settles at 2.20 cm and 2.77 cm, test MAE is 3.52 cm and 3.53 cm, and both runs stop on the patience rule. The logged training loss is computed with dropout on, so it sits above the validation loss even when the restored model is stable.
10. Does either network beat Phase 1? The LSTM beats the mean baseline (4.53 cm) on both flights and loses to Lasso (2.50 cm, $R^2$ 0.756) on both. The CNN loses to the mean baseline as well. Mean LSTM $R^2$ across the two folds is about 0.50.
11. Why might the CNN lose? The predictable part of this label, beyond a nearly constant 1.2 m of integrated bias, is a few centimetres. A 65 ms detector plus pooling has little reason to recover what the hand-made features and the physics prior already summarise, and 450 to 553 windows are enough for it to fit the early part of one flight.
12. What must the test flight not be used for? Batch-norm statistics, the learning-rate schedule, early stopping, and the notebook grid. Prediction files are written only after the best validation checkpoint is restored.
13. Where are the artefacts? `models/dl/E1_*.pt` and `E2_*.pt`, `results/metrics/*_loss.csv`, `results/metrics/imu_e1_e2.csv`, `results/predictions/E1_*.csv` and `E2_*.csv`. Columns are `seq_id`, `window_id`, `y_true`, `y_pred`.
